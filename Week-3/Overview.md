Readings: 

This week's readings were both looking at how archaeological maps aren't just technical tools 
the readings were trying to show how archaeologists actually shape things and how the past gets represented. The first reading was explaining that hand-drawn maps involve a lot of attention to detail and actual skill to make. Digital mapping is much faster and takes much less skill, but it means the actual understanding and art of mapmaking is lost sometimes. The reading also brings up political issues with mapmaking, like the opinions of people making the maps and the power they have with their design. Reading two looks at how maps can change versions of reality, that maps are somewhat like performances, and that maps are only meaningful when people use them. 

Connecting these readings to the GIS lab 
Learning about vector and raster layers and projection errors showed that maps are constructed and not a natural thing, its something people have created for our use. During a lecture, we also talked about analog vs. digital, and that definitely fits into this week's learnings with the idea that digital map tools change how archeologists are able to engage with the world around them. 
From this week I'm taking away that mapping is definitely not just technical and is actually very interpretive and political, based on who is designing them. Just like someone's writings or papers, you need to consider who's made a map and how that could affect the information you're taking from it.
